1: (zero-one-specialization)=
2: # The Zero-One Specialization Problem
3: 
4: Before you read this section, you should understand the basics of
5: dynamic shapes. Make sure you have read the following sections:
6: 
7: * {ref}`dynamic_shapes`
8: * {ref}`torch.export`
9: * {ref}`what_is_a_specialization`
10: 
11: In `torch.compile`, we specialize automatically on inputs with sizes
12: 0 or 1 and assume that any remaining inputs cannot be 0 or 1. This
13: simplifies tasks like contiguity and broadcasting checks, as it
14: avoids adding extra guards. However, this can cause problems for
15: sparse models with many symbolic integers that in practice have
16: tensors of size 0, 1, or 2. For example, consider when you a task is
17: something like collecting likes on page.
18: 
19: While it's possible to stop specializing on 0/1 upfront, executing
20: normal PyTorch code often reintroduces 0/1 guards, as many conditions
21: in PyTorch check for values being 0 or 1. Although models that work
22: for `N > 2` often generalize to `N = 1`, this isn't guaranteed, especially
23: with symbolic variables. For example, in hand tracking, a dimension
24: size of `N = 0`, `1`, or `2` may lead to different graph behaviors.
25: Simply hoping that the `N > 2` model generalizes can expose soundness issues.
26:
